Research

Experimental aerodynamics of bluff bodies and heavy vehicles

My research focuses on the experimental aerodynamics of bluff bodies and heavy vehicles, with emphasis on wake dynamics, pressure drag, flow separation, and passive or active strategies for drag reduction.

The central objective is to connect the underlying flow physics of three-dimensional separated wakes with aerodynamic performance in configurations relevant to ground vehicles.

Research Themes

Bluff-Body Wakes

Near-wake topology, recirculation regions, pressure recovery, wake asymmetry, and reflectional-symmetry-breaking dynamics.

Heavy-Vehicle Aerodynamics

Flow mechanisms relevant to square-back and heavy-vehicle geometries, including crosswind effects and practical drag-reduction constraints.

Flow Control

Passive and active control strategies, including adaptive flaps, base blowing, rear cavities, and self-adaptive devices.

Methods

  • Wind-tunnel experiments: controlled measurements of forces, pressure distributions, wake development, and drag-reduction performance.
  • Particle image velocimetry: two-component and flow-field measurements for characterising separation, recirculation, wake structure, and turbulence.
  • Track testing: aerodynamic assessment under more representative road conditions.
  • Data analysis: linking local flow organisation with global quantities such as drag, base pressure, and aerodynamic loading.
